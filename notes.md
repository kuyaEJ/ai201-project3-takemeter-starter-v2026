# Milestone 1 — Community and candidate distinctions

## Community

**r/WebNovels** — https://www.reddit.com/r/WebNovels/

Why it fits the rules:

- **Public.** No invite or login needed to read it.
- **Not built around one show or release.** Posts cover many different novels, so they're readable without being caught up on any one story. (I switched away from r/ShadowSlave for this reason: it's about a single novel and most posts are full of spoilers.)
- **Lots of text, with real variation in quality.** Some posts are long and argued. Others are one line.

## What I saw while reading

The post types I noticed: favorite novels, general advice, discussions, writing, recommendations, resources, looking for novels, and novel ads (authors promoting their own work). Most posts boil down to either a **discussion** or a **question**.

> Reminder to self: those are *topics*. Topics tell me what a post is *about*. For labels I need things I can *point at* in the text. The distinctions below turn what I saw into checkable features.

## Candidate distinctions

Each one is something I could check by looking at a post, not just a feeling about quality.

1. **Asking vs. telling.** Some posts exist to get an answer ("Looking for a novel where the MC…", "Is X worth reading?"). Others state an opinion or share something. *Check:* is the main point of the post a request or a question?

## Watch out for in Milestone 2

- **Reddit flair is not my taxonomy.** Flairs are the subreddit's own categories, and copying them would skip the design work. I can look at them, but my labels have to come from distinctions like the ones above.
- **Distinctions 1 and 3 might combine into labels.** Possible shapes: question / opinion with reasons / opinion without reasons. I'll decide in M2.
- **Self-promo posts may be easy to spot but rare.** If I make that a label, I need to check there are enough of them (no label over 70%, and enough of the small ones to measure).
